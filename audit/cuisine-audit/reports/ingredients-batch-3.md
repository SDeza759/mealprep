### Nori
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 380, carbs: 44.3, protein: 41.4, fat: 3.5
- **Source:** Mainstream sushi-nori labels (gimMe, One Organic, Sushi Chef: 5–10 cal per 2.5–3g sheet ⇒ ~400/100g) reconciled with the Japanese Standard Tables value for yaki-nori (297 kcal/100g on an available-carbohydrate basis)
- **Category:** Condiments & Oils
- **Flesh meat?:** no
- **Unit-based?:** yes — 1 standard sheet ≈ 2.8g (half-sheet 1.4g)
- **Note:** Raw-vs-dried trap: USDA "Seaweed, laver, raw" (35 kcal/100g) is wet laver, ~10x lower than the dried sheets you actually buy — do not use it. The 380 figure is stated US-label style (fiber counted at 4 cal) so 4C+4P+9F stays under the calorie number; Japanese tables list 297 because they exclude the large fiber fraction. Either way a sheet is only ~10 cal.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Canned Tuna
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 107, carbs: 0, protein: 23.2, fat: 0.9
- **Source:** As-sold packaged product — StarKist / Bumble Bee Chunk Light in water, drained (2 oz / 56g drained = 60 cal, 13g protein, 0.5g fat)
- **Category:** Canned & Legumes
- **Flesh meat?:** yes
- **Unit-based?:** no — but a standard 5 oz can drains to ~110g, useful for grocery rounding
- **Note:** Drained-weight basis; do not use the undrained can weight. USDA's own entries straddle this figure (SR Legacy 175159 = 116 cal / 25.5P; newer Foundation Foods 334194 = 90 cal / 19P), so the label value was taken as the middle and most representative. Packed-in-oil versions roughly double calories and fat; solid white albacore is ~128 cal / 26P.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Dry Sherry
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 116, carbs: 1.4, protein: 0.2, fat: 0
- **Source:** Standard composition for dry (fino/manzanilla) sherry, ~15% ABV — UK CoFID "Sherry, dry"; no direct USDA SR entry for dry sherry
- **Category:** Condiments & Oils
- **Flesh meat?:** no
- **Unit-based?:** no
- **Note:** This is drinking-quality dry sherry, NOT supermarket "cooking sherry" — that is a separate, salted and sweetened product (~83 cal, 10g carbs, very high sodium per 100g). Most of the calories are alcohol and largely cook off.
- **Confidence:** medium

### Grape Leaves
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 69, carbs: 12.5, protein: 4.5, fat: 2.1
- **Source:** USDA FDC 169393 (Grape leaves, canned) — the jarred/brined product that is actually sold
- **Category:** Canned & Legumes
- **Flesh meat?:** no
- **Unit-based?:** yes — 1 drained leaf ≈ 4g (a dolma uses roughly one leaf)
- **Note:** Jarred-in-brine basis, drained. USDA also lists raw grape leaves at 93 cal / 17.3 carbs (FDC 168575) — do not use that unless the leaves are genuinely fresh-picked. Brine makes these very high sodium; rinse before use.
- **Confidence:** high

### Lamb Leg
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 230, carbs: 0, protein: 17.9, fat: 17.1
- **Source:** USDA SR Legacy "Lamb, domestic, leg, whole (shank and sirloin), separable lean and fat, trimmed to 1/4in fat, choice, raw"
- **Category:** Meat & Seafood
- **Flesh meat?:** yes
- **Unit-based?:** no
- **Note:** Raw, lean-and-fat basis — consistent with the existing `Ground Lamb` row (250 cal). A well-trimmed boneless leg is leaner; USDA lean-only is 128 cal / 20.6 protein / 4.5 fat, so a butcher-trimmed roast lands between the two. If a recipe calls for bone-in leg, the bone is ~25% of purchase weight and the grocery quantity should be scaled up.
- **Confidence:** medium

- **Category:** Condiments & Oils

### Tteok
- **Type:** `registry`
- **Macros per 100g raw:** calories: 230, carbs: 50, protein: 4, fat: 0.5
- **Source:** Mainstream packaged Korean rice cake labels (Han Asia ~237 cal/100g; generic garaetteok 230 cal / 50C / 4P / 0.5F per 100g)
- **Category:** Grains & Pasta
- **Flesh meat?:** no
- **Unit-based?:** no — sold by weight in vacuum packs; a tteokbokki portion is ~150g
- **Note:** As-sold basis (already-steamed rice cake), NOT dry rice — it does not absorb much more water when cooked, so no dry-to-cooked rescale applies here. Softer fresh/refrigerated tteok is wetter and can run ~170 cal/100g, so brand variance is roughly ±25%.
- **Confidence:** medium
